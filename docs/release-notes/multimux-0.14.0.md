# multimux 0.14.0

_Released 2026-10-31._

### Added
- `tests/hdr10plus_route.rs`: HDR10+ over a route fed the Tears of Steel TS fixture (issue #1428): `SUPPLEMENTAL-CODECS` `/cdm4` + `VIDEO-RANGE=PQ`, all 259 HDR10+ SEI byte-identical in the served fMP4, ffprobe HDR10+ side data on every frame, and `mediastreamvalidator` findings equal to the same stream without the SEI.
- Dolby Vision profile 5, 8.1 and 8.4 routes (issue #1428): the fMP4 init carries `dvcC`/`dvvC` (and `dvh1` for profile 5) and the multivariant playlist carries the Dolby Vision `CODECS`/`SUPPLEMENTAL-CODECS`/`VIDEO-RANGE`; RPU NAL units pass through unchanged.
- HDR10 routes (issue #1428): an HEVC HDR10 MPEG-TS input now yields an fMP4 init with `colr` + `mdcv` + `clli` and a multivariant playlist with `VIDEO-RANGE=PQ` (HLG: `HLG`; SDR stays omitted), with `CODECS` taken from the real SPS (`hvc1.2.4.L153.90`, via the `transmux` codec-string fix). Pinned by `tests/hdr10_route.rs` against `private/fixtures/hdr10/meridian-hdr10-60f.ts`, with ffprobe, GPAC `MP4Box -diso` and Apple `mediastreamvalidator` as external oracles (they skip when absent); the ffprobe check strips all in-band SEI so only the boxes can supply the values.
- WebVTT subtitle renditions on a low-latency route now publish real partial segments (issue #1592). Each subtitle part is cut where the video part is cut and has the same duration, and is a complete WebVTT document: the `WEBVTT` line, the same `X-TIMESTAMP-MAP` as the segments and the cues displayed during that part. The subtitle playlist lists `EXT-X-PART-INF`, `EXT-X-PART` and `EXT-X-PRELOAD-HINT` like the video playlist, and blocking reload with `_HLS_msn` and `_HLS_part` waits for the requested subtitle part.

### Fixed
- A mid-stream PMT change keeps each teletext subtitle page on its own rendition: pages are matched to the declared renditions by identity (page number, language, hearing-impaired flag), never by position, so a dropped or reordered page no longer serves one language under another rendition's `EXT-X-MEDIA`, and a page with no declared rendition is not served. `SubtitleServing` gained `identity`.
- When two teletext pages share a language and hearing-impaired flag, every one after the first is declared without `AUTOSELECT=YES`, so AUTOSELECT=YES renditions keep distinct LANGUAGE/CHARACTERISTICS combinations (RFC 8216bis §4.4.6.1.1). Hearing-impaired pages stay `AUTOSELECT=YES` (Apple HLS authoring spec 4.6 is a MUST).
- A syntactically invalid `Range` (`bytes=5-3`, `bytes=-`) is ignored and the full body served with `200`, not answered `416` or a whole-body `206` (RFC 9110 §14.1.1, §14.2).
- The video `PART-TARGET` follows the segmenter's frame-grid target (issue #1621), so non-terminal partial segments stay within 85% to 100% of it at 23.976, 25, 29.97, 30, 50 and 59.94 fps; `PART-HOLD-BACK` stays three times the largest target across renditions. `mediastreamvalidator` no longer reports `#EXT-X-PART non-terminal partial segment duration must be at least 85% of PART-TARGET` on the Tears of Steel route, and `tests/hdr10plus_route.rs` requires its absence.
- RTSP, RTP and WHIP video inputs with B-frames now carry a monotonic DTS no later than the PTS (issue #1611, via `transmux::RtpStreamDepacketiser`), so their fragmented-MP4/TS outputs get real composition offsets instead of `dts == pts` in decoding order.
- The reconnect schedule's equal jitter is strictly below the raw delay (`[raw/2, raw)`, as documented): `Duration::mul_f64` rounds to the nearest nanosecond, so a top-of-band draw on a raw delay under about 16 ms could round up to exactly the raw delay. Its test `a_long_life_resets_the_backoff` (issue #1617) also no longer treats a delay observed on the millisecond-rounded paused clock as out of band.
- A Playlist blocking reload (`_HLS_msn`) that the stream never satisfies now answers `503` after the blocking timeout on the media, I-frame, audio and subtitle playlists and the TS-HLS playlist, as RFC 8216bis section 6.2.5.2 says ("SHOULD return Service Unavailable, such as HTTP 503"). It answered `404`, which the validator reports as a missing playlist.
- Split-audio followers drop audio samples that precede the shared origin even when they arrive after the first leader cut fixed it. They used to be packed into the first audio part, which lasted 0.352 s against a 0.2 s Part Target Duration on the france2 capture (issue #1599, RFC 8216bis section 4.4.4.9).
- Split-audio playlists carry their own `PART-TARGET`: the route's part target rounded up to a whole number of audio frames (0.224 s for 32 ms E-AC-3 frames against 0.2 s), so no audio Partial Segment exceeds it (RFC 8216bis section 4.4.4.9). `PART-HOLD-BACK` follows the largest `PART-TARGET` and `EXT-X-SERVER-CONTROL` stays identical on every playlist (sections 4.4.3.8 and 6.2.4). Apple `mediastreamvalidator`'s level-4 'Partial Segment duration exceeds PART-TARGET' finding on every audio playlist is gone (issue #1603).
- Split-audio playlists resolve their resources from the playlist directory (see `hls-runtime`), and the validator tests now mirror them that way (issue #1599).
- On a route without split audio or subtitles the I-frame playlist's rendition report for the media playlist now carries the media playlist's live edge including `LAST-PART` (issue #1595, fixed in `hls-runtime`); the media playlist itself has no required report (RFC 8216bis Appendix B.1).
- A low-latency playlist (video, split audio, WebVTT subtitles) no longer lists an `EXT-X-PART` with `DURATION=0` (issue #1597): the subtitle parts copied the leader's part durations, and the leader's final part of a segment had a zero duration when only audio or teletext samples remained at the cut (fixed in `transmux`, which now merges them into the part before the cut). Every playlist with an `EXT-X-PART` also carries an `EXT-X-PRELOAD-HINT` right after a segment closes (issue #1577, fixed in `hls-runtime`).
- The WebVTT subtitle playlists of a low-latency route now carry the same `EXT-X-SERVER-CONTROL` attributes and values as the video playlist (RFC 8216bis section 6.2.4) plus `EXT-X-PART-INF`, so Apple's `mediastreamvalidator` no longer reports "Missing EXT-X-PART-INF tag" or "Missing server control attribute" three times on them (issue #1589).

### Changed
- The subtitle followers no longer hold a segment back for up to one target duration waiting for its open cues to close. A segment or part is published at the video cut. A cue that is still on screen at that moment is written with its end clamped to the end of the part or segment and written again, with its original start, in each following part and segment until it closes, when its real end is used (RFC 8216bis section 3.1.4). A cue whose closing teletext data arrives after the part or segment that contains its real end was published keeps the clamped end in that copy.
- Route `audio_profiles` HE-AAC tracks now use the output sample rate as their media timescale (`mdhd` 44100 for a 22050 Hz core, 2048-tick AAC frames) in every fMP4 output.

### Added
- WebVTT subtitle renditions from DVB teletext (issue #1428): once the track set shows a teletext PID, every page its PMT teletext descriptor lists as a subtitle page (type 0x02) or a subtitle page for hearing impaired people (type 0x05) is declared as an `EXT-X-MEDIA TYPE=SUBTITLES` rendition in group `subs` (`LANGUAGE` from the ISO 639-2 code as BCP 47, `NAME` the language plus ` (SDH)` for type 0x05 and the page number where two names would clash, `AUTOSELECT=YES` for hearing-impaired pages, Apple's hearing-impaired `CHARACTERISTICS`, `DEFAULT=NO`), with `SUBTITLES="subs"` on every `EXT-X-STREAM-INF` and `wvtt` in `CODECS`. Each is a sibling LL-HLS playlist (`subs-{n}.m3u8`, up to 8 per program) of `.vtt` segments served as `text/vtt` with the primary's target duration and low-latency setting, in the rendition reports of every playlist. Subtitle segment N covers the span of video segment N with the same media sequence number, including when it has no cue. A segment is published when every cue overlapping it has closed, or once the video's media time has passed its end by one target duration; a cue still open then is written with the segment end as its end and again, with its original start, in the next segment. Each segment starts `WEBVTT` / `X-TIMESTAMP-MAP=LOCAL:00:00:00.000,MPEGTS:0` (the fMP4 timeline starts at zero at the timeline origin the split-audio followers share) and cue times are the cue PTS minus that origin. New route field `subtitles` (default `true`; `false` leaves the master and segments as they were). `RouteHandle::with_subtitles`, `RouteHandle::subtitle_servings`, `SubtitleServing`. Apple's `mediastreamvalidator` adds only its low-latency tag findings for these playlists (`EXT-X-PART-INF`, part-related `EXT-X-SERVER-CONTROL` attributes: a WebVTT playlist has no partial segments yet). **Not yet:** routes with a `ts_hls` output get no subtitles (only fMP4 routes do). **Breaking:** `Route` is not `#[non_exhaustive]`, so code that builds it with a struct literal must add `subtitles` (use `true` for the default behaviour); deserialised configs are unaffected (`#[serde(default)]`). New dependency `caption-convert` (feature `teletext`).
- Closed captions in the multivariant playlist (issue #1428): the per-program segmenter looks for A/53 caption data in H.264/HEVC SEI and MPEG-2 picture user data while the route runs, and once found the master advertises one `EXT-X-MEDIA TYPE=CLOSED-CAPTIONS` per stream (field 1 -> `CC1`, field 2 -> `CC3`, CEA-708 services -> `SERVICEn`) with `CLOSED-CAPTIONS="cc"` on every `EXT-X-STREAM-INF`. The master claims no captions before the first detection and is byte-identical for a route with none. New route field `closed_caption_overrides` (`{"instream_id":"CC1","name":"English","language":"en"}`, validated in `Config::validate`) sets `NAME`/`LANGUAGE` per stream; it never advertises a stream the video lacks. `RouteHandle::with_closed_caption_overrides` / `note_closed_captions`. **Breaking:** `Route` is not `#[non_exhaustive]`, so code that builds it with a struct literal must add `closed_caption_overrides` (use `Vec::new()`).
- Route `audio_profiles` (issue #1428): per-track `"he-aac"` / `"he-aac-v2"` declaration (`track` is an `index` or `track_id` selector) so an ADTS HE-AAC track gets explicit hierarchical SBR / SBR+PS signalling in its fMP4 init `AudioSpecificConfig` and `mp4a.40.5` / `mp4a.40.29` in the HLS `CODECS`, instead of the implicit AAC-LC `mp4a.40.2`. Applied where tracks leave the `Trunk` for an init segment or master playlist; wins over the PMT `AAC_descriptor`. HE-AAC v1 is reachable only through this declaration (a v1 descriptor value is also valid for plain AAC-LC, EN 300 468 Table 26 note 7); transmux itself honours only an HE-AAC v2 descriptor. New public items: `config::AudioProfile`, `config::AudioProfileOverride`, `Route::audio_profiles`, `RouteHandle::with_audio_profiles`. Adding the `Route::audio_profiles` field means a `Route` struct literal must name it (`Vec::new()` keeps today's behaviour); deserialised configs are unaffected (`#[serde(default)]`). Output is byte-identical when neither the declaration nor a PMT descriptor says HE-AAC.
- Split-audio routes now expose `#EXT-X-RENDITION-REPORT` between sibling renditions (issue #1428 slice 4); Apple `mediastreamvalidator` no longer reports "Missing rendition report".
- Split-audio routes serve a correct multivariant playlist (issue #1428 slice 3): per-group `CODECS` including the audio codec, `BANDWIDTH`/`AVERAGE-BANDWIDTH` summing video and audio, and `LANGUAGE`/`NAME`/`CHARACTERISTICS`/`CHANNELS` derived from the PMT when the route's `audio_renditions` leave them unset (config values win). `declared_bandwidth_bps` applies to the video part only. The primary playlist's `CODECS` no longer names audio mapped to a separate rendition. Config validation accepts empty `language`/`name` on a route with `audio_rendition_sources`.
- Split-audio followers now cut audio parts on the leader's part boundaries, so each audio playlist's live edge (MSN, part) tracks video's within one part; blocking reload on an audio part unblocks as soon as the matching audio part is published. Rendition reports show audio LAST-PART equal to video's within one part.

### Changed (BREAKING: `dash_pull` runs on `dash-runtime`)
- **`dash_pull` is a host over `dash_runtime::client::DashClient`.** The tokio loop and the media-plane mapping stay; MPD parsing, segment addressing, the UTC clock, refresh, the live join, availability gating, failure classification and track selection are the core's. Every observable difference is listed here and, with the reasons, in `docs/dash-runtime/DESIGN.md` section 12.3.
  - **Clock.** `jiff::Timestamp::now()` is gone from the pull path (a guard test keeps every clock read out of `source::dash_pull`; time comes from one `HostClock` seam). The host UTC clock is anchored at open and every 600 s; for a live MPD the ladder is `UTCTiming` (`direct`, `http-xsdate`, `http-iso`, `http-head`, `http-ntp`) over the host clock over the MPD response `Date`. A dynamic MPD without `availabilityStartTime`, or with no usable clock source, now ends the attempt with a typed error (it used to start at `@startNumber`).
  - **Join point and early requests.** A live pull joins `suggestedPresentationDelay` (else the larger of 4 s and three maximum segment durations) behind the UTC live edge and delivers that much media at once; it never requests a segment before its availability start. A `425`, or a `404`/`410` within 1 s of availability, is retried at 250 ms doubling to 1 s without counting; the old fixed 500 ms retry of any live `404` (240 times, about two minutes) is gone. A `404`/`410` inside the window is a counted failure.
  - **Failures.** Per resource, consecutive failures back off 0.5 s doubling to 4 s. A segment is given up after 4 (a static presentation ends the attempt; a live one skips it, marks a discontinuity and counts `multimux_pull_fragment_abandoned_total`), an init or `sidx` after 4, MPD refreshes after 8 in a row; a refresh that does not parse is retried instead of ending the attempt. The first MPD failing or not parsing, and any `401`, still end the attempt at once.
  - **Periods and tracks.** Every Period plays, in order (it was the last Period only), and the sample timeline is stitched across Period boundaries and gaps so a Period that restarts its clock does not run time backwards in the `Trunk`. Per Period one video AdaptationSet (Role `main`, else the first) at its highest Representation and one audio AdaptationSet play, where it was the first Representation of every AdaptationSet; text, MPEG-2 TS, WebM, trick-mode and protected sets are dropped with a logged reason. Protected content with nothing else to play is `MultimuxError::Encrypted`.
  - **Addressing and requests.** `SegmentList`, `SegmentBase` + `sidx` with byte-range requests, `BaseURL` alternates at every level, `$Bandwidth$`, `S@n`, `@endNumber` and `urn:mpeg:dash:urlparam:2014` now work. The MPD refresh is conditional (`If-None-Match`/`If-Modified-Since`, `304` absorbed) and follows `minimumUpdatePeriod` with a 1 s floor; a redirect of the MPD is the base of its relative URLs. `UTCTiming` requests never carry credentials. The MPD body cap is 4 MiB (64 MiB before) and a window of 4 segment requests replaces one per Representation.
  - **Cross-origin URLs are refused by default.** Every URL an MPD makes the server request (`BaseURL`, segments, `UTCTiming`) must have the MPD's own origin, so a remote manifest cannot make a server-side pull fetch an internal address; a Representation that leaves it is dropped. An MPD served from one host with segments on a CDN needs `"allow_cross_origin": true` on the route.
  - **Metrics.** No metric was added or renamed; `multimux_pull_fragment_abandoned_total` now counts the segments the core gives up on.
- **API.** `InputSpec::DashPull` gains `allow_cross_origin` (serde default `false`; a constructor or exhaustive pattern without `..` breaks). `source::dash_pull`: removed `DashAction`, `DashResourceId`, `RepIndex` and `DashIngestSession::with_clock`; `DashIngestSession` is now fed `DashInput` and hands out `dash_runtime::client::Action`, with `set_wall_clock(mono_us, unix_us)` in place of the clock closure; added `DashPullRoute::with_cross_origin_urls`, `dash_config`, `pull_limits`, `FetchTarget`, `PROGRAM`. New dependency: `dash-runtime`.

### Fixed (DASH-pull adoption)
- Samples of a fragment whose `trun` carries sizes only now have their real duration and sync flags: `transmux::Fmp4Demux` takes them from the init movie's `trex` (see the `transmux` changelog). GPAC's multi-Period output played through `dash_pull` had every sample of such a fragment at one `dts`.

### Fixed (round-9 audit F1/F3)
- **`ts_hls` no longer serves an empty playlist forever for an H.264 stream with no IDR picture.** The `idr_only` default (Apple HLS Authoring Spec 7.4) stays the preferred cut rule, but after `IDR_FALLBACK_TARGETS` (3) target durations, at least `IDR_FALLBACK_MIN_SECS` (6 s), of IDR-less video the start gate replays the stream from the oldest non-IDR sync sample it saw and the segmenter cuts on non-IDR random-access points for the rest of the stream. New per-route option `Route::ts_hls_idr_fallback` (`"auto"` default, `{"targets": N}`, `"never"` = the old strict behaviour). Either way the operator is told once: a `warn` log, the counter `multimux_ts_hls_non_idr_total{route,mode}` and the admin route status field `ts_hls_idr` (`"fell_back"` / `"awaiting"`, `null` while IDR-aligned). A route that is IDR-aligned reports and counts nothing.
- **A PMT-declared PID that never carries a packet no longer holds every TS output back until 4 MiB of input.** `TsIngestSession` now calls `StreamingTsDemux::release_silent_heads` once one second (`SILENT_PID_SETTLE`) of arrival time has passed, so a low-bitrate SPTS starts in about a second instead of tens of seconds or never. A silent PID that sends later is announced by a later track change.
- `ts_hls` segment `EXTINF` now matches the segment's audio and video content (transmux: the segmenter holds a cut for lagging audio); verified with Apple's `mediastreamvalidator` through the full route.

### Fixed (round-9 audit F4/F5)
- A finite source no longer reconnects forever. An `hls_pull`/`dash_pull`/`smooth_pull` route whose playlist or manifest ends cleanly (`#EXT-X-ENDLIST`, a static MPD or Smooth manifest) used to be re-pulled about three times a second (47 segment GETs/s against the origin), each re-publish rejected as a "second concurrent publisher". The route is now marked `HealthState::Ended` (new variant, label `ended`), keeps serving what it published, and the supervisor does not reconnect it. `GET /readyz` and `multimux_route_up` count an `Ended` route as serving, like a `loop: false` file route.
- The supervisor releases a route's publisher slots when an attempt ends, so a reconnect is never rejected as a concurrent publisher; the backoff only restarts from the floor after an attempt stayed live for at least 30 s (`HEALTHY_LIFE`), so a source that goes live and drops within seconds backs off 1, 2, 4 s up to the cap instead of retrying every 250-500 ms.
- A live `hls_pull`/`dash_pull`/`smooth_pull` route now produces closed segments on its outputs. A pulled segment delivers all of its samples in one fetch, which overran the 64-entry timed ring before the segmenter could drain it; the cursor reported `Lagged`, the samples were lost, and no segment ever closed. Pull inputs now use a 2048-entry timed ring (`source::pull_trunk_config`), and a segmenter that lags logs a warning instead of dropping samples silently. Covered by `tests/hls_pull_live.rs` (a sliding-window live origin and a finite playlist, both cut from a real fixture).

### Changed
- TS-over-HTTP ingest (`source::ts_http`) now consults the shared `tshttp-runtime` policy core (new dependency): a finite response (`Content-Length` / 206 `Content-Range`) that ends before all declared bytes arrived is a failed `Connect` ("truncated transfer") instead of a clean `Ended`, and the post-establishment stall (same threshold, `ingest_read_timeout_secs`, same error text) is decided by the core. Reconnect/backoff stays with the route supervisor; the core's start timeout is disabled. Redirect and auth behaviour is unchanged. Covered by `tests/ts_http_policy.rs` and the `source::ts_http` unit tests.

### Added
- `ts_hls` outputs now cut segments on IDR pictures only by default (Apple HLS Authoring Spec 7.4, issue #1425): new per-route option `Route::ts_hls_cut` (`"idr_only"` default, `"any_rap"` restores the previous open-GOP-tolerant rule; unknown values are rejected by serde), `RouteHandle::with_ts_cut_policy`. Open-GOP-only sources (no IDR at all) therefore produce no `ts_hls` segments unless `any_rap` is set. fMP4/CMAF outputs always cut on any random-access point. The start gate opens on the first IDR under `idr_only`.
- A `ts_hls` output refuses HEVC and AV1 (Apple HLS Authoring Spec 1.5): the program is not segmented and the typed `MultimuxError::UnsupportedCodec` text is exposed as `RouteHandle::output_error()` and as `output_error` in the admin route status. This is a runtime check (codecs are only known once a program's tracks arrive); `Config::validate` cannot know them.
- `Route::declared_bandwidth_bps: Option<u64>` (serde, default `None`) and `RouteHandle::with_declared_bandwidth_bps(...)` — operator-declared peak bitrate wired into `HlsOriginBuilder::declared_bandwidth_bps`. Master playlist requests before the first measurable segment now return `503 Service Unavailable` with `Retry-After: 1` instead of a fabricated `BANDWIDTH`.


### Fixed
- A TS-over-HTTP route whose connection sent bytes but never produced a program (a PAT with no PMT, a PAT trickling forever) was never bounded by the handshake deadline, because the driver is `Live` from the first byte: silence was only caught after `IngestTimeouts::read` and a trickle never. The shared policy core's start timeout is now the route's `IngestTimeouts::connect` and its `sampled` input is "a program was announced", so such a route fails with `ts/http handshake timed out: no media within <connect>` and the supervisor reconnects. Behaviour change: a TS stream that takes longer than the connect budget to announce its first program now fails instead of waiting.
- HLS pull input no longer stalls forever on an `#EXT-X-KEY:METHOD=AES-128` playlist (issue #1477). `run_hls_pull` now fetches every `Action::FetchKey` through the same authenticated GET path as segments (credentials stay scoped to the configured origin; redirect policy unchanged), bounds a key body at 4096 bytes, treats a non-2xx, transport error, oversized body or non-16-byte body as a failed fetch (`HlsClient::on_key_error`, then the existing bounded retry), and feeds a good key to `HlsClient::on_key`. A segment that does not decrypt (`DecryptFailed`) and any unsupported encryption (`SAMPLE-AES`, encrypted `EXT-X-MAP`, key without URI) end the session with the typed `MultimuxError::LlHls(..)` error instead of stalling or passing ciphertext through. `HlsFetchId` gains a `Key(KeyId)` variant and `HlsIngestSession::key_fetch_failed`. Covered by `tests/hls_pull_keys.rs`.

- `parse_segment_filename` now accepts the real instance form `seg-{track}-{instance}-{seq}.m4s` in addition to the legacy `seg-{track}-{seq}.m4s` (issue #1422). As a result `inject_segment_events` (DASH SCTE-35 `emsg` splice) and the in-progress chunked whole-segment fallback now actually run for real HLS/DASH filenames instead of only the unit tests' short names. `iframe-seg-…` URIs are deliberately excluded so I-frame byte ranges never drift. Box-scan assertions in `tests/origin_iframe.rs` cover the new behaviour.


### Changed
- LL-HLS media playlists now carry `HOLD-BACK` (3 x Target Duration, RFC 8216bis §4.4.3.8) in `#EXT-X-SERVER-CONTROL`; Apple's `mediastreamvalidator` rejects a low-latency playlist without it. Wire-visible: `media.m3u8` grows by `,HOLD-BACK=<3 x target>` (golden `origin_response_headers` content-length 248 -> 261). Remaining validator findings on the live origin are tracked in #1429.
- HTTP origin resource responses now serve fragmented-MP4 segments and parts (`.m4s`) as `video/iso.segment` instead of `video/mp4` (Apple HLS authoring spec 10.4); init segments (`init-*.mp4`) stay `video/mp4` and MPEG-TS segments stay `video/mp2t`. Wire-visible: DASH clients fetching `.m4s` from the shared resource route also see the new type — DASH players use the MPD `@mimeType`, not the response header.

### Added
- DASH-IF Live Media Ingest listener now streams a `Segment` request body as CMAF chunks (P3b): `Begin`/`Chunk`/`Finish` jobs over the pump, with per-object byte cap and total body timeout preserved; `IngestError::SegmentInProgress` (409) on concurrent same-representation segments.
- DASH-IF Live Media Ingest HTTP listener (`InputSpec::DashifIngest`): axum catch-all POST/PUT handler, bounded channel to a per-publishing-point pump task owning `DashifIngestSession`, status mapping, publishing-point prefix enforcement. (P2b-1)
- DASH-IF Live Media Ingest sans-IO ingest session (`DashifIngestSession`, `IngestObject`) under `dashif_ingest::session`: per-Representation init bytes + `local_to_global` track-id remap in MPD order, `Established`/`NewProgram`/`Sample` events, buffered flush, `lmsg`/`slat` handling.
- Live HLS playlists (`media.m3u8`, `iframe.m3u8`, and TS-HLS `media.m3u8`) now carry `#EXT-X-PROGRAM-DATE-TIME` by default (Apple HLS authoring 8.4 MUST): `Config::program_date_time` defaults to `"arrival"` (wall-clock at arrival, sampled once per origin; an explicit trunk time anchor still wins). Set `"off"` to restore the previous output. `RouteHandle::with_program_date_time(...)` exposes the same knob for non-config routes.
- `ProgramSegmenter` now start-gates on the first video sync (IDR) sample (Apple HLS authoring 7.4 MUST, issue #1425): for programs whose track set contains a video track, timed samples (all tracks) are dropped until the first video sync sample, which opens the stream; audio-only programs are not gated. Dropped samples never pin the media anchor. The gate re-arms only when the segmenter is rebuilt for a changed track set.
- LL-HLS output now serves an I-frame (trick-play) playlist at the fixed path `/iframe.m3u8` and advertises it in `/master.m3u8` via `#EXT-X-I-FRAME-STREAM-INF` when available (issue #1413 P3); `_HLS_part` on `/iframe.m3u8` is rejected with `400`.
- HTTP origin now delivers HLS playlists (and DASH MPDs) with `Content-Encoding: gzip` when the client sends `Accept-Encoding: gzip` (Apple HLS authoring spec 10.1): tower-http `CompressionLayer` (new `compression-gzip` feature) gated by a response content-type predicate so only `application/vnd.apple.mpegurl` / `application/dash+xml` responses are compressed; media segments, init segments and parts stay byte-exact (no `Content-Encoding`, no `Accept-Ranges` removal). `Vary` carries both `Origin` and `Accept-Encoding`. `gzip;q=0` and unknown encodings (e.g. `br`) yield the identity body.
- HTTP origin resource responses (`init-*.mp4` / `seg-*.m4s` / `part-*.m4s`) now honour `Range: bytes=...` (RFC 9110 §13.1.5, §14.1-14.4, §15.3.7, §15.5.17): a single satisfiable byte range returns `206 Partial Content` with a matching `Content-Range`/`Content-Length`, unsatisfiable ranges return `416` with `Content-Range: bytes */<total>`, suffix and open-ended ranges are supported, multiple ranges and non-`bytes`/invalid ranges (and any request carrying `If-Range`) are answered with `200` and the full body, and every response advertises `Accept-Ranges: bytes`. This is the P0 prerequisite for serving I-frame playlist `EXT-X-BYTERANGE` entries. Range is applied to the final (emsg-spliced) bytes.
